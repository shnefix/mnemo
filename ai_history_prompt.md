# NotebookLM prompt: the history of AI as a story

Turn the sources into a narrated story called "The Long Road to Thinking Machines": the history of artificial intelligence from 1943 to 2024.

Structure it as a story in chapters, not a list of facts:
1. The Dream (1943–1959): artificial neurons, the Turing test, Dartmouth, the first programs.
2. Hype and the First Winter (1958–1980): the perceptron, ELIZA, Shakey, the "Perceptrons" book, the Lighthill Report.
3. Rules and Boom (1980–1995): expert systems, Japan's Fifth Generation project, backpropagation, the second winter.
4. Quiet Builders (1989–2009): LeNet, TD-Gammon, SVMs, LSTM, Deep Blue, deep belief networks, ImageNet.
5. The Deep Learning Explosion (2011–2017): Watson, AlexNet, word2vec, DQN, GANs, ResNet, AlphaGo, the Transformer.
6. The Age of Language Models (2018–2024): BERT and GPT, GPT-3, AlphaFold 2, text-to-image, ChatGPT, GPT-4 and open models, the Nobel Prizes, the EU AI Act.

Style: a dramatic, cinematic narrator. Treat the winters as low points and the comebacks as turning points. Keep every date and name accurate to the sources and keep each chapter to about 2 minutes. Explain jargon in one plain sentence. End with a reflection on what the pattern of hype, disappointment and breakthrough suggests about what comes next.

Visual style (for slides, video or infographic output): dark background, near-black navy or charcoal (#0B0F1A), with light text. Use glowing cyan and electric violet accents, a thin horizontal timeline that stays visible across chapters, and a subtle neural-network or circuit motif. Use icy blue tones for AI winter chapters and warm amber for breakthroughs. No white backgrounds.
